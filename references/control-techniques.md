# Control techniques: choose for the player's actual task

Use this as a candidate menu with [game-feel selection](./game-feel-selection.md), not a recipe to install every assist. First reproduce the reported miss and trace raw input, acceptance, state transition, motion, and visible response. Repair dropped events, frame-order errors, latency spikes, or collision defects at their cause; a grace window cannot compensate for a control path that intermittently loses input. Identify whether a proposed change affects **presentation (P)**, **input acceptance (I)**, **controller motion (M)**, or **game rules (R)**. Measure tuning in the game's own time, distance, speed, and device units; the dimensions below are not recommended constants. Check intentional difficulty, competitive fairness, accessibility options, and replay/network determinism where relevant.

## Input intent and timing

| Player symptom or intended feeling | Candidate mechanism | Tune and limits | Observable check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A jump pressed just before landing disappears. | **Landing input buffer (I):** remember the press until the next eligible grounded state; consume it once when the action starts. | Tune lifetime in milliseconds or simulation ticks. Expire on timeout, death, mode change, or incompatible action; never replay a consumed press. Unsuitable when a pre-landing press must have a distinct meaning. | Press at several offsets before contact; see exactly one jump in-window and none after expiry or scene change. |
| A ledge departure and jump feel simultaneous, but the jump fails. | **Coyote window (I/R):** keep jump eligibility briefly after leaving valid support. | Tune time since last support in ticks. Reset only on genuine support reacquisition; do not refresh it from wall contact, moving-platform overlap, or every airborne frame. It widens a precision rule. | Walk off and press at increasing delays; verify the boundary, single use, and no infinite airborne reset. |
| A press during recovery is forgotten despite clear intent to act next. | **Next-action queue (I):** retain one eligible command through recovery and fire at the first legal transition. | Tune queue lifetime and which actions can replace or cancel it. Reject stale targets and state-changing commands; unsuitable when precise recovery timing is the challenge. | Press once during recovery, change target or cancel, and inspect the resulting single action and its target. |
| Simultaneous attack, dodge, and interact inputs produce inconsistent outcomes. | **Explicit action priority (I/R):** resolve competing commands with a documented order or context-specific arbitration. | Tune priority by state and input device, including whether a held input may repeat. This changes which action wins, so preserve deliberate combos and competitive rules. | Replay the same simultaneous inputs across frame boundaries and devices; observe the same result. |
| A quick tap is mistaken for a charge or a hold is accepted too late. | **Tap/hold separation (I):** decide on press, release, or a hold threshold, with clear pending feedback. | Tune threshold in milliseconds and cancellation behavior. Delaying the tap action adds latency; unsuitable where taps must fire immediately unless separate controls are available. | Test taps on both sides of the threshold, interruption, and low frame rate; count one intended command each time. |
| A turn-based player commits a move before understanding its consequences. | **Preview then commit (P/I):** show the selected destination, path, cost, or target before a separate confirm input. | Tune preview persistence and cancellation path, not combat odds. Extra confirmation slows frequent safe actions; use it only for costly or ambiguous decisions. | Change selection, cancel, and confirm; the committed command matches the last visible preview. |

## Movement and traversal

| Player symptom or intended feeling | Candidate mechanism | Tune and limits | Observable check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Movement starts or stops abruptly, or feels slippery. | **Acceleration, braking and reversal shaping (M):** approach the intended speed with distinct acceleration, release braking or reversal rates. | Tune speed, acceleration and braking in world units per second and per second squared. Excessive easing delays control; instant reversal may erase intended weight. Preserve the requested speed and stopping limits. | Compare starts, releases and reversals over equal elapsed time at different update rates; measure stop distance and time to target speed. |
| A grounded character jitters or detaches on traversable slopes. | **Ground adhesion / slope handling (M/R):** maintain valid support using bounded probes or the native controller's floor handling. | Tune probe distance, slope limit and eligible movement states. Do not snap a deliberate jump down, attach to ceilings or traverse forbidden slopes. First fix collision or step-order defects. | Traverse seams and slopes, jump from a slope, and walk off a ledge; confirm valid support without unintended attachment. |
| A near-edge jump clips an overhead corner and loses all height. | **Jump corner correction (M):** try a small collision-safe lateral displacement while rising, then continue upward. | Tune maximum displacement in world units and eligible collision normals. Do not move through solid walls or turn a ceiling into an unintended route. | Approach both corner sides at varied speeds; inspect displacement, clearance, and blocked cases. |
| A horizontal dash catches a ledge lip although the path looks clear. | **Dash/ledge corner correction (M/R):** probe a short upward clearance near the lip before resolving the impact. | Tune vertical probe distance and speed/angle eligibility. It can grant access to higher routes; exclude hazards and deliberate barriers. | Dash into lip and full-height wall; only the intended near miss climbs. |
| Wall jumps fail when the sprite appears to touch the wall. | **Wall proximity window (I/R):** allow the wall action within a small distance of a valid wall, including a brief separation window if appropriate. | Tune distance in world units and separation time in ticks. Check direction and wall identity; broad windows may attach across gaps. | Sweep approach distances and directions around each wall type; confirm no jump from an unrelated surface. |
| The jump apex passes too quickly to steer toward a landing. | **Apex gravity shaping (M):** reduce effective gravity near the apex, optionally only while jump remains held. | Tune vertical-speed band, gravity multiplier, and duration. Changes flight time and reachable geometry; avoid it in ballistic puzzles or synchronized hazards without redesign. | Compare trajectories and landing ranges for tap/hold, at normal and variable frame times. |
| Every jump has the same arc despite short and long press intent. | **Variable jump height (M):** on release, shorten upward travel by changing upward velocity or gravity. | Tune release cutoff speed and acceleration in world units per second. It changes route reachability and can make accidental early release punitive. | Plot peak height against hold duration; confirm monotonic, controllable arcs and stable collision handling. |
| Air steering feels absent or twitchy. | **Air acceleration and braking (M):** set separate acceleration and deceleration while airborne. | Tune acceleration, maximum horizontal speed, and reversal time in physical units. Strong control erases committed momentum; weak control may defeat the intended landing task. | Jump with neutral, held, and reversed input; compare displacement and stop time to the intended feel. |
| Racing steering is nervous at speed or unresponsive in tight turns. | **Speed-sensitive steering (M):** map input to yaw rate or steering angle as speed changes; filter only genuine device noise. | Tune angle/rate versus speed and input response time. Too much filtering adds lag; too little low-speed authority blocks hairpins. | Drive the same slalom at several speeds with tap and held steering; inspect overshoot and achievable turn radius. |

## Aiming, dragging, and physical controls

| Player symptom or intended feeling | Candidate mechanism | Tune and limits | Observable check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A stick drifts at rest or small aim corrections do nothing. | **Radial deadzone and response curve (I/M):** suppress centered noise, then remap remaining magnitude continuously. | Tune deadzone as a fraction of stick travel and curve slope. Large deadzones lose precision; axial deadzones can make diagonals uneven. Offer calibration where hardware varies. | Log raw and mapped axes at rest, near center, and around a full circle; check drift and smooth diagonals. |
| A near-target stick aim feels needlessly fragile. | **Aim slowdown/friction (M):** lower reticle angular speed near an eligible target while input is active. | Tune target cone, distance, and slowdown strength. It can resist moving off a target and alter competitive outcomes; expose a toggle or mode policy. | Sweep across targets and empty space at equal stick input; compare speed, exit effort, and disabled behavior. |
| A fast directional input misses an obvious discrete target. | **Directional snap (I/R):** choose a valid target near the input direction at command time, using visible selection criteria. | Tune angular cone, range, and tie-breaking. Snapping changes target selection, so avoid hidden retargeting in competitive play; provide toggle/policy where relevant. | Test two close targets, occlusion, equal angles, and moving targets; selected target matches the visible rule. |
| Dragged objects jump under the cursor at pickup. | **Preserve grab offset (M):** retain the initial local contact point instead of moving the object's center to the pointer. | Tune any smoothing in screen/world units per second; smoothing can lag a fast drag and penetrate obstacles. Physics handles need constraint limits. | Grab at edges and center, move slowly and quickly, release; inspect contact continuity and collisions. |
| Most of a charge/drag range gives either too little power or an abrupt maximum. | **Charge/power response curve (I/M/R):** map the existing hold time or pull distance to force with readable correspondence. | Tune the normalized curve and dead travel while retaining declared maximums unless force changes are in scope. Do not add charging delay to an immediate action; a nonlinear curve changes fine-control demands. | Sample short, medium and full input; compare displayed power to applied force, test cancellation and ensure the same input yields the same result. |
| A touch drag begins while the player only meant to tap or scroll. | **Gesture intent threshold (I):** delay drag capture until displacement or direction distinguishes it from a tap/scroll. | Tune movement in physical pixels or device-independent units and maximum decision time. Larger thresholds make dragging sluggish; respect platform scrolling conventions. | Try short taps, slow drags, diagonal swipes, and scrolls on each device; see one correct gesture. |
| A thrown or steered physics object behaves differently at low frame rates. | **Sampled release velocity (M/R):** estimate throw velocity from recent pointer/controller motion, then clamp to the game's physical limits. | Tune sampling window, velocity cap, and filtering in time and world units. Too much smoothing erases flicks; authoritative physics may require synchronized samples. | Repeat comparable gestures at varied frame rates; compare release speed, direction, and trajectory. |

The published [*Celeste & Forgiveness* examples](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html) illustrate coyote time, jump buffering, apex gravity, corner correction, and wall-jump proximity in one particular platformer. They establish possible mechanisms, not universal benefits or tuning targets. Other rows are design candidates to validate against the actual game and devices, not claims that a cited game uses them.
