# Feedback, camera, audio, and play-flow techniques

Use the affected sections with [game-feel-selection.md](game-feel-selection.md). Entries are candidate mechanisms, not mandatory features or universal prescriptions. They apply only to actual or requested mechanics. Tune existing systems first; distinguish cosmetic expression from changes to rules and control. Timing values need project measurements and trials, not a fixed cross-genre recipe.

## Impact and motion

| Situation / candidate | Mechanism and useful tuning | Limits and observable check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact passes unreadably — **hit stop** | Briefly hold selected animation or, when scoped and supported, simulation. Tune duration in ms/ticks, affected entities, impact threshold, and overlap policy. | Trace clocks and collider alignment. Frequent contacts must not accumulate permanent slowdown; input and recovery must remain defined. Check contact onset and return in playback, plus actual online agreement if simulation changes. |
| A confirmed hit is hard to locate — **hit flash / silhouette pulse** | Short local material/outline response at the struck object; tune contrast, duration and cooldown. | Preserve team/status colors and avoid repetitive flashing. Confirm the target is distinguishable at gameplay scale, including reduced effects and non-color cues. |
| An existing action is hard to anticipate — **telegraph / anticipation pose** | Show the real direction, affected region or preparation state using the game's vocabulary. Tune cue lead time and prominence against the actual actionable event. | Do not add input delay to immediate actions or imply a larger/smaller hit region. A longer warning may alter difficulty. Check cue-to-impact correspondence and cancellation. |
| Motion feels rigid — **squash/stretch or pose exaggeration** | Brief deformation of a presentation child or authored pose; tune axis, amplitude and recovery to the intended material. | Unsuitable for deliberately rigid silhouettes or where deformation obscures collision. Check collision/selection bounds, attachment points and reset under interruption. |
| A body or tool stops unnaturally — **follow-through / secondary motion** | Overlap visual recovery, recoil or settling with permitted control; tune damping, amplitude and interruptibility. | Animation must not imply unavailable actions or block the next input without design intent. Repeated use should settle without drift or snapping on cancel. |
| Force direction is unclear — **directional reaction / knockback** | Local pose recoil can communicate direction; actual displacement uses the gameplay controller/physics. Tune direction, impulse or visual offset separately. | Real knockback changes positioning and may cause ring-outs. Do not add it as cosmetic work. Check the contact normal, legal displacement, ownership and final outcome. |
| Fast movement is hard to follow — **trail / afterimage** | Encode recent direction with short-lived, low-density history. Tune length, lifetime and emission by distance/time. | Trails can hide hazards or suggest an active damage area. Check clean silhouettes, teleport/reset cleanup and crowded scenes; omit when tracking is already clear. |
| A major contact lacks local weight — **contact mark / local particles** | Place a small surface response at the confirmed contact point, with material-appropriate behavior. Tune size, lifetime and per-event cap. | Do not imply destruction or damage absent from the rules. Check surface alignment, event deduplication and object reuse; preserve the next target and outcome. |

Use hit stun, cancel windows or invulnerability only when that mechanic is explicitly within scope; they alter action eligibility, not merely impact presentation. More force, shake or freeze is not automatically more satisfying. Establish the contact and its cause before adding supporting channels.

## Camera

| Situation / candidate | Mechanism and useful tuning | Limits and observable check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small motions constantly drag the view — **dead/soft zones and damping** | Follow only outside a defined zone, then converge at a controlled rate. Tune zone in screen space, damping and maximum lag. | Too much damping delays hazard visibility; some genres require exact tracking. Test starts, reversals, stops, teleport and camera reset. |
| The player cannot see the route ahead — **look-ahead / directional framing** | Bias framing toward meaningful travel, aim or intent. Tune lead distance, activation threshold and return rate. | Velocity alone can point the view away during knockback or reversals. Check both travel directions and slow aiming; avoid revealing hidden information. |
| Actor and target cannot be read together — **target-group framing** | Fit the meaningful participants and endpoint while maintaining minimum readable scale. Tune margins, zoom limits and target priority. | Do not constantly recenter to irrelevant entities or make every actor tiny. Check target loss, crowded scenes, split screens and different aspect ratios. |
| A strong event needs directional emphasis — **camera impulse / kick** | Add a decaying offset oriented by the event; tune translation/rotation, decay and aggregate cap separately. | Preserve aiming and accessibility settings. Check rapid events do not accumulate drift or move the aiming ray unintentionally; restore the correct camera after interruption. |
| Speed or climax needs brief emphasis — **zoom / FOV modulation** | Small, bounded changes tied to a meaningful event or speed. Tune range, onset and recovery, with an off/reduced path. | Affects peripheral visibility, distance judgment and comfort. Do not use it for every action. Compare readability during transitions and ensure exact baseline recovery. |
| Scenery hides a required actor — **camera obstruction handling / occluder fade** | Use the native collision/fade system to retain actor visibility. Tune clearance, fade threshold and hysteresis. | Do not expose opponents/rooms hidden by game rules. Test tight spaces and repeated occluder entry for flicker and camera snapping. |

For an engine with a suitable existing camera module, prefer its event-based impulse and blending support. Unity's [Cinemachine Impulse documentation](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineImpulse.html) illustrates source/listener separation and directional, time-shaped signals; it is not a requirement to add Unity or Cinemachine to another stack.

## Audio and haptics

| Situation / candidate | Mechanism and useful tuning | Limits and observable check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact is muddy or weightless — **transient/body/tail layering** | Assign a clear onset, a supporting body and an appropriate friction/decay tail using available sounds. Tune relative gain, duration and event alignment. | Layering is optional; one good sound may suffice. Audition at comparable loudness on target speakers, check clipping, and distinguish first contact from sustained sliding. |
| Music or many sounds hide the action — **ducking / voice priority** | Briefly reduce competing channels or limit low-priority voices. Tune depth, attack/release, voice count and event priority. | Frequent events must not permanently silence music or essential warnings. Listen to a crowded sequence and recovery, not just an isolated sample. |
| Repeated actions sound mechanical — **bounded variation / material mapping** | Select among appropriate samples or modest pitch/gain variations; map confirmed surface/action states when available. | Preserve recognizable gameplay meanings and avoid random variation that communicates nonexistent outcomes. Use cosmetic randomness; listen to repetitions and quiet settings. |
| Important touch/controller events lack tactile acknowledgement — **event-shaped haptics** | Use supported motors/patterns with distinct importance and duration; respect settings and per-device support. | Never make haptics the only signal. Repeated impulses need a cap; verify on the physical device and report simulation-only checks honestly. |

Camera bob, shake, blur, automatic FOV changes and moving UI can affect comfort and reading. Preserve appropriate alternatives and settings; consult [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) for applicable motion guidance. This does not prescribe a visual style or claim that every motion effect is harmful.

## Comprehension and flow

| Situation / candidate | Mechanism and useful tuning | Limits and observable check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A puzzle/strategy action has an unclear consequence — **legal-action / placement / trajectory preview** | Show the known proposed state or path before committing. Tune update cadence and visual distinction between preview and confirmed state. | Respect fog, randomness and authority; label uncertainty rather than guaranteeing an outcome. Check preview versus actual result and cleanup on cancel or invalid target. |
| A cascade or chain is hard to follow — **causal sequencing / local emphasis** | Emphasize each meaningful change in its actual order, with enough overlap to retain pace. Tune spacing and grouping by causality. | Do not reorder contacts, scores or removals or hold gameplay merely to fit effects. Check that players can trace cause to result and repeated cascades stay concise. |
| Too many instructions compete with the next action — **contextual guidance / progressive disclosure** | Reveal the instruction or control when useful; retain discoverable expert access. Tune trigger and dismissal conditions. | Do not hide required rules, change terminology, or repeatedly interrupt experienced play. Check first use, returning users, skipped guidance and failure recovery. |
| Failure makes experimentation expensive — **quick retry / short reset path** | Reuse the existing checkpoint or run-reset boundary and remove unnecessary repeated steps. Tune transition length and preserved selections. | Check reset of timers, score, inputs, camera and rewards; do not silently alter checkpoint/ranking rules. Measure time to actionable retry if claiming a reduction. |
| A reversible local decision causes excessive fear of misclick — **undo / cancel / reversible preview** | Retain a bounded prior state or defer commitment until a clear action. Tune scope and history depth only as needed. | Requires actual reversibility; not a generic refund for purchases, rewards or competitive turns. Check exact restoration, repeated undo and state after a new branch. |
| A valid placement/action is difficult to confirm — **snap/settle acknowledgement** | A short local alignment, contact cue or state transition confirms the committed action. Tune settling separately from the logical snap threshold. | Do not move a collider to decorate success or imply success before validation. Check invalid placement, interrupted drag and rapid repeated placements. |
| Many small achievements obstruct play — **notification coalescing / event priority** | Group redundant notices and prioritize actionable results. Tune grouping window, maximum visible count and expiry. | Keep exact rewards, important failures and accessible feedback available. Check duplicate events, delayed receipts and backlog clearing on scene exit. |
| A milestone or failure lacks closure — **outcome sequence with a repeat path** | Connect final state, recognition, consequence/reward and next action; allow an appropriate short repeat/skip path. Tune control return and emphasis by event importance. | Victory must follow the actual win condition. Skips must preserve receipts/state, and failure must not use celebration cues. Check first view, repeats and exit during the sequence. |

For cozy/care games, candidates such as settling motion, gentle material sound and contextual acknowledgement may fit an existing interaction; combat-style recoil need not. For rhythm games, changing input windows, latency offsets or judgement changes scoring and must follow the requested rules and measured calibration. For competitive strategy, prediction/undo may expose information or change fairness. Use the same situational selection process rather than assigning a preset effect bundle by genre.

These tables are design candidates authored for this workflow. The linked sources explain particular mechanisms or accessibility considerations; they do not establish universal tuning values, player preference, or that this entire library has been playtested.
